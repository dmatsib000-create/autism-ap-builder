1. APPLIED BEHAVIOR ANALYSIS (ABA) THERAPY
ABA/behavioral support is recommended based on the following documented functional impairments: severe noncompliance; emotional dysregulation; expressive language delays; receptive language delays; sensory processing difficulties impacting regulation and participation; significant adaptive impairment. Functional Behavior Assessment (FBA) recommended to identify the functions of specific target behaviors and guide individualized programming. Goals should be functional, meaningful in daily life, and coordinated with the school team.
  • Parent/caregiver training: Caregiver-implemented intervention is a required and evidence-based component of ABA services; specific training content is determined by the treating BCBA and commonly spans the function of target behaviors, reinforcement strategies in natural routines, generalization of clinic and school skills to the home, and crisis response for high-intensity behaviors. Parent training should be documented as a distinct billable service, not subsumed under child direct therapy hours.
  • Least restrictive and assent-based practice: We support programming that uses the least restrictive, most naturalistic procedures appropriate to the child's profile, seeks patient assent as developmentally appropriate, honors and documents assent withdrawal, and relies on positive-reinforcement-based strategies as first-line, with restrictive procedures reserved for documented justification and oversight, consistent with BACB ethical standards.
  • Treatment modality: Naturalistic Environment Teaching (NET) and functional skill-based programming should be the primary treatment modality for most targets. Discrete Trial Training (DTT) is appropriate for initial skill acquisition in structured teaching contexts; skills acquired via DTT must be generalized to natural settings.

2. PARENT-CHILD INTERACTION THERAPY (PCIT)
Recommended for: noncompliance, ADHD, anxiety.
Evidence-based, parent-mediated intervention improving the parent-child relationship while reducing disruptive behaviors. Referral to a PCIT-trained therapist is recommended.
  Note: PCIT is clinically distinct from the parent training component of ABA. PCIT targets the parent-child relationship through structured in-session coaching (Child-Directed Interaction and Parent-Directed Interaction phases), with a primary goal of improving relational attunement and consistent, contingent limit-setting. ABA parent training targets caregiver implementation of behavioral reinforcement strategies in naturalistic routines to generalize skill acquisition. When both are in place, goals and billable sessions should be documented as non-overlapping.

5. OCCUPATIONAL THERAPY (OT)
  • Sensory processing: Comprehensive sensory evaluation using standardized tools (Sensory Profile 2 and/or Sensory Processing Measure-2 [SPM-2]; SPM-2 provides separate home and school forms directly applicable to IEP planning). Individualized sensory support plan developed for home, school, and community environments. Targets: hypersensitivity.
  • Seating and positioning: OT assessment of seating, postural support, and classroom ergonomics. Appropriate positioning is a prerequisite for sustained attention, fine motor output, and sensory regulation; inadequate seating is a modifiable barrier to academic participation.
  • ABA/OT collaboration for sensory-related behavior: Sensory-driven behaviors should be assessed collaboratively by OT (sensory integration framework) and the ABA team (functional behavior analysis). A behavior that appears sensory-driven may have operant functions (escape, attention, access to tangibles) that require behavioral intervention in addition to sensory supports. Coordinated assessment prevents siloed treatment and avoids targeting sensory behaviors without understanding their function.
  • Service frequency: OT services at a frequency to be determined following formal evaluation, typically 1-2 sessions per week for direct intervention. School-based and clinic-based OT address different goals; concurrent services are appropriate when goals are documented as distinct.
