They have received a diagnosis of Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD) (ICD-10-CM: F84.0), classified as Level 2 (requiring substantial support), confirmed at the evaluation referenced above. In addition, they carry the following co-occurring diagnoses, each of which contributes to the educational challenges described below: Attention-Deficit/Hyperactivity Disorder, combined presentation (ADHD); an anxiety disorder.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
EDUCATIONAL IMPACT
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
These conditions result in the following challenges that require specialized support in the educational setting:
  • Social understanding: Difficulty with social comprehension, perspective-taking, and peer interaction that limits meaningful participation in group academic and social activities.
  • Behavior: Difficulty with emotional regulation and behavioral compliance that interrupts instruction for this student and peers.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
REQUEST FOR IEP ELIGIBILITY EVALUATION AND/OR 504 REVIEW
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
I understand that {Student Name} currently has a 504 Plan in place. Based on the current evaluation, I am requesting that the team consider: (1) updating the 504 Plan to reflect current functional needs, and (2) evaluating whether {Student Name} meets eligibility for an Individualized Education Program (IEP) under IDEA, which provides more comprehensive and legally enforceable services and supports than a 504 accommodation plan.

Given the identified level of support needed, a 504 accommodation plan alone may be insufficient to provide Free and Appropriate Public Education (FAPE) as defined under IDEA and Florida Rule 6A-6.03028(1). I recommend that the school evaluate {Student Name}'s eligibility under the Autism Spectrum Disorder eligibility category (34 C.F.R. §300.8(c)(1); Florida Rule 6A-6.03023).

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
RECOMMENDED SERVICES
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
If eligible for special education services, I recommend the IEP include the following:

Counseling and Social-Emotional Support Services: School-based counseling is recommended to address the social-emotional and behavioral regulation needs documented above. Goals: anxiety management strategies (graduated exposure, relaxation techniques, cognitive coping) adapted for the school setting; social skill generalization in the school environment, reinforcing goals targeted in specialized social skills instruction.

Low Student-to-Teacher Ratio Classroom: A small group or self-contained classroom setting with a low student-to-teacher ratio is recommended. The structured, lower-stimulation environment of a small classroom supports sensory regulation, individualized instructional pacing, and behavioral consistency that is not reliably achievable in a general education classroom of typical size without highly intensive support.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
RECOMMENDED ACCOMMODATIONS
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
I recommend that the IEP team consider the following accommodations, calibrated to evaluation findings and individual educational need. These are clinical recommendations, not legal entitlements; the team's selection should reflect what the evaluation supports.

Core ASD-Related Accommodations:
The following accommodations have broad evidence support across the autism spectrum. Implementation should be calibrated to this student's developmental level and individual presentation.
  • Predictable daily schedule with advance written or visual notice before transitions (schedule format and complexity calibrated to developmental level)
  • Visual supports (format calibrated to developmental level and literacy): pictorial schedule and visual transition cues for younger or non-reading students; written agenda, task checklists, or digital planner for older or verbal students — visual representation of schedules, task steps, and expectations reduces transition anxiety and supports regulation across the autism spectrum

Behavioral and Emotional Accommodations:
  • Attention: extended time on assessments and lengthy assignments, preferential seating away from distractors, chunked assignments with check-ins, movement breaks
  • Anxiety: designated calm-down space, graduated support for anxiety triggers, flexible deadlines for anxiety-driven avoidance, advance notice before schedule changes
