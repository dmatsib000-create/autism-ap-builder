EDUCATIONAL IMPACT

  • Communication: Significant difficulty with verbal and nonverbal communication, including understanding classroom instructions, expressing needs, and participating in peer conversation and group activities.

RECOMMENDED SERVICES
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
I recommend the following updates to the current IEP:

Speech-Language Pathology (SLP): School-based SLP services are recommended to address the documented communication needs that affect participation in classroom routines, play, and early learning activities. Recommended goals include: expressive language development: expanding sentence length and grammar, asking and answering wh- questions, and talking about familiar and recent events with adult support; receptive language: following one- and two-step directions within classroom routines, understanding early concepts such as position, size, and quantity (in, on, big, little, more), and responding to simple questions about familiar activities and shared books; social-pragmatic language: sharing attention and interests with adults and peers (joint attention), starting and responding to interactions, turn-taking in play and conversation, and participation in group activities; speech intelligibility and articulation targeting sound patterns, relative to developmental expectations, that make speech harder for teachers and peers to understand; language disorder: evaluation and treatment of receptive and/or expressive language deficits affecting comprehension, participation in classroom routines and early learning activities, and peer interaction, addressed as a diagnosis distinct from ASD-related social communication impairment.

Occupational Therapy (OT): School-based OT services are recommended to address sensory, fine motor, and/or adaptive needs that affect participation in classroom routines, play, and early learning activities. Goals: fine motor and pre-writing development: grasp and manipulation of age-appropriate tools and materials such as crayons, scissors, blocks, and fasteners, and imitation of pre-writing strokes and simple shapes; motor planning and praxis: imitation of new motor actions, sequencing of multi-step play and classroom routines, and use of simple tools; adaptive self-care skills relevant to the school day: toileting, handwashing, dressing tasks such as jackets and shoes, and snack and mealtime routines.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
RECOMMENDED ACCOMMODATIONS
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
I recommend that the IEP team consider the following accommodations, calibrated to evaluation findings and individual educational need. These are clinical recommendations, not legal entitlements; the team's selection should reflect what the evaluation supports.

Core ASD-Related Accommodations:
The following accommodations have broad evidence support across the autism spectrum. Implementation should be calibrated to this student's developmental level and individual presentation.
  • Predictable daily schedule with advance written or visual notice before transitions (schedule format and complexity calibrated to developmental level)
  • Visual supports (format calibrated to developmental level and literacy): pictorial schedule and visual transition cues for younger or non-reading students; written agenda, task checklists, or digital planner for older or verbal students — visual representation of schedules, task steps, and expectations reduces transition anxiety and supports regulation across the autism spectrum

Individually Indicated Accommodations:
  • Motor accommodations: adapted materials (for example, easy-grip crayons, adapted scissors, a slanted work surface), extra time and hands-on support for fine motor tasks, and support for gross motor and playground participation, with physical therapy or adapted physical education consultation if the team finds it indicated

Behavioral and Emotional Accommodations:
  • Communication: AAC device access if applicable, visual and written directions in addition to verbal instructions, SLP consultation for classroom communication accommodations
  • Language: simplified verbal instructions, extended response time
