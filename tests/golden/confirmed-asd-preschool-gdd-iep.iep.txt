I am a pediatrician at the UF Behavior and Development Clinic. I evaluated {Student Name} on {Date of Evaluation} and am writing to provide a formal medical recommendation in support of the family's request for special education services under the Individuals with Disabilities Education Act (IDEA).

Specifically, I am requesting a comprehensive multidisciplinary evaluation to determine eligibility for special education services. Florida allows 60 calendar days from written parental consent for that evaluation, with the exclusions the rule provides. The findings supporting that request are set out below, together with the services and accommodations I recommend.

He has received a diagnosis of Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD) (ICD-10-CM: F84.0), classified as Level 2 (requiring substantial support) (with Global Developmental Delay, formal evaluation underway), confirmed at the evaluation referenced above.

Educational eligibility should not be assigned on the basis of this physician letter alone. Global developmental delay is suspected but not yet confirmed, and the district's evaluation should include a comprehensive standardized developmental assessment; brief developmental screeners are not sufficient to establish the degree of delay. At this age, a developmental evaluation is more informative than an IQ-based cognitive battery or academic achievement testing.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
EDUCATIONAL IMPACT
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
These conditions result in the following challenges that require specialized support in the educational setting:
  • Communication: Significant difficulty with verbal and nonverbal communication, including understanding classroom instructions, expressing needs, and participating in peer conversation and group activities.
  • Motor skills: Motor difficulties affecting handwriting, physical education participation, and completion of classroom tasks requiring fine or gross motor coordination.
  • Adaptive skills: Difficulty with daily living and self-care skills that affect independent functioning within the school setting.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
REQUEST FOR SPECIAL EDUCATION EVALUATION
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Based on the above diagnosis and identified educational impact, I strongly recommend that {Student Name} receive a comprehensive evaluation for special education eligibility under IDEA, including consideration of the Autism Spectrum Disorder eligibility category (34 C.F.R. §300.8(c)(1); Florida Rule 6A-6.03023) and any additional eligibility categories supported by the evaluation results.

Because global developmental delay is suspected at this evaluation, I also ask the team to consider eligibility under the Developmental Delay category (34 C.F.R. §300.8(b); Florida Rule 6A-6.03027), which Florida makes available for children ages three through nine, or through completion of grade 2, whichever occurs first. Evaluating for both categories in a single evaluation spares the family a second referral if the team does not find {Student Name} eligible under the Autism category.

The evaluation should be a comprehensive developmental evaluation across cognitive, communication, physical, social or emotional, and adaptive development, using a standardized developmental instrument (e.g., the Battelle Developmental Inventory 3rd ed. or the Mullen Scales of Early Learning), together with assessment of behavioral functioning and sensory processing.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
RECOMMENDED SERVICES
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
If eligible for special education services, I recommend the IEP include the following:

Speech-Language Pathology (SLP): School-based SLP services are recommended to address the documented communication needs that directly affect classroom access. Recommended goals include: AAC system evaluation and implementation (low-tech: core vocabulary boards, PODD; and/or high-tech: speech-generating device) with training for all school communication partners; expressive language development: sentence formulation, topic maintenance, and narrative organization; receptive language: following multi-step classroom directions, understanding figurative language, and processing academic vocabulary.

Occupational Therapy (OT): School-based OT services are recommended to address sensory, fine motor, and/or adaptive needs that affect classroom participation and academic output. Goals: fine motor and graphomotor development: pencil grip, letter formation, and/or keyboarding as a compensatory strategy for written output; adaptive self-care skills relevant to the school day: cafeteria routines, clothing management, and bathroom independence.

Developmental Evaluation: A comprehensive developmental evaluation is recommended in place of a standard psychoeducational battery. At this age, a standardized developmental instrument (e.g., the Battelle Developmental Inventory 3rd ed. or the Mullen Scales of Early Learning) covering cognitive, communication, motor, social-emotional, and adaptive development is more informative than IQ or academic achievement testing. Results will directly inform IEP goal development and the Developmental Delay eligibility determination (Florida Rule 6A-6.03027).

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
RECOMMENDED ACCOMMODATIONS
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
I recommend that the IEP team consider the following accommodations, calibrated to evaluation findings and individual educational need. These are clinical recommendations, not legal entitlements; the team's selection should reflect what the evaluation supports.

Core ASD-Related Accommodations:
The following accommodations have broad evidence support across the autism spectrum. Implementation should be calibrated to this student's developmental level and individual presentation.
  • Predictable daily schedule with advance written or visual notice before transitions (schedule format and complexity calibrated to developmental level)
  • Visual supports (format calibrated to developmental level and literacy): pictorial schedule and visual transition cues for younger or non-reading students; written agenda, task checklists, or digital planner for older or verbal students — visual representation of schedules, task steps, and expectations reduces transition anxiety and supports regulation across the autism spectrum

Individually Indicated Accommodations:
  • Motor accommodations: keyboarding as alternative to handwriting, extended time on written tasks, adapted physical education referral if indicated

Behavioral and Emotional Accommodations:
  • Communication: AAC device access if applicable, visual and written directions in addition to verbal instructions, SLP consultation for classroom communication accommodations
