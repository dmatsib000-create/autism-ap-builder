RECOMMENDATIONS FOR PRIMARY CARE from Developmental-Behavioral Pediatrics (DBP)

DBP follow-up:
  • We will keep seeing the patient, next in 3 months to recheck mood and safety. Between our visits, you manage the items in this list. Thank you for sharing this patient's care.

Actions:
  • PCP (next visit): Ask whether ABA, speech-language therapy, psychotherapy, and a social skills group have started. If not, ask where the family is on each waitlist. The insurance case manager can find in-network providers, and families often have to call a waitlist more than once.
  • PCP (next visit): Check that these referrals are scheduled or done: clinical genetics and child psychiatry. We placed every referral. Some insurance plans want the referral to come from primary care; if so, please re-send it. You do not need to order genetic testing: clinical genetics orders it and follows up the results.
  • PCP (now): Start planning the move to adult health care with the family: adult doctors, support for decision-making, and benefits.
  • PCP (next visit): Recheck weight after the medication change
  • Family: Bring the school progress report to the next visit

Contact DBP before the return visit if:
  • School, insurance, disability services, or the move to adult care needs updated diagnostic testing or documentation.
  • A new developmental, learning, attention, or mood concern needs a diagnostic evaluation.
  • Depression or anxiety is not improving with treatment, or is worsening.

Urgent, or for another service:
  • If suicidal thoughts come up:
      - Intent, a plan, or an attempt: arrange an emergency evaluation the same day.
      - Thoughts without intent or a plan: do the BSSA at that visit. Make a written safety plan with the patient and family, and ask the family to lock up firearms and medications.
      - If the BSSA calls for a mental health evaluation within 72 hours, options in our area are: voluntary psychiatric evaluation at UF Psychiatric Hospital (Vista), Meridian, or SMA in other counties; Charlie Health (telehealth intensive outpatient therapy and psychiatry, usually a quick intake, age 11 and older); or a Community Action Treatment (CAT) team, for a child at high risk of hospitalization who does not meet criteria for involuntary admission (352-505-3534; myflfamilies.com/catt).
      - Give every patient who screens positive the toolkit's patient resource list. The family can call or text 988 at any time.
  • New mania or psychosis: arrange a mental health evaluation the same day (the emergency department if none is available). Substance use that complicates treatment: tell child psychiatry promptly.
  • The patient loses language, social, or motor skills they already had: refer to pediatric neurology.
