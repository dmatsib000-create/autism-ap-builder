ABA-based services are the most extensively studied and supported intervention for young children with ASD or suspected ASD. Recommended intensity to be determined by the treating BCBA following individualized skills assessment; services should span home, clinic, and natural-environment settings, with caregiver coaching embedded throughout and coordination with Early Intervention services.
Families who have access to Naturalistic Developmental Behavioral Intervention (NDBI) programs (e.g., Early Start Denver Model, Pivotal Response Training, JASPER) should engage or continue with these approaches. NDBI-based ABA carries a particularly strong evidence base for children in the infant-toddler-preschool period and represents a preferred style of early intervention when accessible.
  • Parent/caregiver training: Caregiver-implemented intervention is a required and evidence-based component of ABA services; specific training content is determined by the treating BCBA and commonly spans the function of target behaviors, reinforcement strategies in natural routines, generalization of clinic and school skills to the home, and crisis response for high-intensity behaviors.   • Least restrictive and assent-based practice: We support programming that uses the least restrictive, most naturalistic procedures appropriate to the child's profile, seeks patient assent as developmentally appropriate, honors and documents assent withdrawal, and relies on positive-reinforcement-based strategies as first-line, with restrictive procedures reserved for documented justification and oversight, consistent with BACB ethical standards.
