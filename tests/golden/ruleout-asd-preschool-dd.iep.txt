I am a pediatrician at the UF Behavior and Development Clinic. I evaluated {Student Name} on {Date of Evaluation} and am writing to provide a formal medical recommendation in support of the family's request for special education services under the Individuals with Disabilities Education Act (IDEA).

Specifically, I am requesting a comprehensive multidisciplinary evaluation to determine eligibility for special education services. Florida allows 60 calendar days from written parental consent for that evaluation, with the exclusions the rule provides. The findings supporting that request are set out below, together with the eligibility categories I would ask the team to consider and the services and accommodations I recommend.

She was referred to this clinic for evaluation of concerns in the following areas: social communication and interaction; adaptive functioning; motor development. A comprehensive diagnostic evaluation was completed at the visit referenced above. Autism Spectrum Disorder was formally considered, and full DSM-5 diagnostic criteria are not met at this evaluation; an autism diagnosis is not being assigned. A single unifying medical diagnosis has not been established.

Ruling out autism does not settle the question of eligibility. The functional impairments described below were directly observed at this evaluation and stand on their own. Under IDEA, eligibility rests on whether the district's evaluation identifies a disability that adversely affects educational performance and creates a need for specially designed instruction; a medical diagnosis is neither required for that determination nor sufficient to establish it (34 C.F.R. §300.8). I hope the findings below are useful to the team, whose evaluation will draw on sources I do not have, including the school's own observations of this student across the day.

Speech-language pathology, occupational therapy, and physical therapy are related services available under any eligibility category (34 C.F.R. §300.34). The communication and motor findings described here support those services whichever category the team determines, and the absence of an autism diagnosis does not bear on them.

Educational eligibility under the Intellectual Disability category should not be assigned on the basis of this physician letter alone. A comprehensive psychoeducational evaluation is required per IDEA evaluation procedures and should include a comprehensive standardized cognitive battery, a standardized adaptive behavior measure (e.g., Vineland-3, ABAS-3, SIB-R, DABS, or BASC-3 adaptive composite), and academic achievement testing.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
EDUCATIONAL IMPACT
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
The findings below affect educational performance and require specialized support in the school setting:
  • Communication: Significant difficulty with verbal and nonverbal communication, including understanding classroom instructions, expressing needs, and participating in peer conversation and group activities.
  • Motor skills: Motor difficulties affecting handwriting, physical education participation, and completion of classroom tasks requiring fine or gross motor coordination.
  • Adaptive skills: Difficulty with daily living and self-care skills that affect independent functioning within the school setting.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
REQUEST FOR SPECIAL EDUCATION EVALUATION
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
{Student Name} presents with documented functional impairments that affect educational performance, described in the Educational Impact section above. Autism has been ruled out; the need for evaluation has not. IDEA's child find requirement reaches a student suspected of having a disability even when that student is advancing from grade to grade (34 C.F.R. §300.111(c)(1)), so passing grades need not delay the evaluation.

The evaluation should include assessment of: cognitive abilities, academic achievement, adaptive behavior, communication skills, behavioral and social-emotional functioning, and sensory-motor development.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
ELIGIBILITY CATEGORIES FOR THE TEAM TO CONSIDER
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
To help the district assign the right evaluators, the following eligibility categories are the ones this evaluation supports considering. The determination itself belongs to the team; this is clinical input, not a conclusion about eligibility.

  • Language Impaired (Florida Rule 6A-6.030121): documented receptive and/or expressive language needs affecting access to instruction. Florida evaluates language and speech eligibility separately, so both may be worth considering.
  • Developmental Delay (Florida Rule 6A-6.03027): Florida makes this category available for children ages three through nine, or through completion of grade 2, whichever occurs first.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
RECOMMENDED SERVICES
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
If eligible for special education services, I recommend the IEP include the following:

Speech-Language Pathology (SLP): School-based SLP services are recommended to address the documented communication needs that directly affect classroom access. Recommended goals include: expressive language development: sentence formulation, topic maintenance, and narrative organization; receptive language: following multi-step classroom directions, understanding figurative language, and processing academic vocabulary.

Occupational Therapy (OT): School-based OT services are recommended to address sensory, fine motor, and/or adaptive needs that affect classroom participation and academic output. Goals: adaptive self-care skills relevant to the school day: cafeteria routines, clothing management, and bathroom independence.

Psychoeducational Evaluation: A comprehensive psychoeducational evaluation is recommended to fully characterize the academic achievement and cognitive processing profile. The evaluation should include: cognitive assessment (IQ and index scores), academic achievement testing across reading, mathematics, and written expression (WIAT-4 or equivalent), phonological processing assessment (CTOPP-2 if reading concerns are present), working memory and processing speed indices, and a Specific Learning Disability (SLD) eligibility determination per Florida Rule 6A-6.03018. Results will directly inform IEP goal development and eligibility determination.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
RECOMMENDED ACCOMMODATIONS
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
I recommend that the IEP team consider the following accommodations, calibrated to evaluation findings and individual educational need. These are clinical recommendations, not legal entitlements; the team's selection should reflect what the evaluation supports.

Structure and Predictability Accommodations:
The following accommodations are supported by the specific findings documented in this evaluation. Implementation should be calibrated to this student's developmental level and individual presentation.
  • Visual supports (format calibrated to developmental level and literacy): pictorial schedule and visual transition cues for younger or non-reading students; written agenda, task checklists, or digital planner for older or verbal students — visual representation of schedules, task steps, and expectations reduces transition anxiety and supports regulation for students with communication, attention, and self-regulation needs

Individually Indicated Accommodations:
  • Motor accommodations: keyboarding as alternative to handwriting, extended time on written tasks, adapted physical education referral if indicated

Behavioral and Emotional Accommodations:
  • Communication: AAC device access if applicable, visual and written directions in addition to verbal instructions, SLP consultation for classroom communication accommodations
