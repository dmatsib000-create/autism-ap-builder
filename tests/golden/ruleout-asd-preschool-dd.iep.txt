I am a pediatrician at the UF Behavior and Development Clinic. I evaluated {Student Name} on {Date of Evaluation} and am writing to provide a formal medical recommendation in support of the family's request for special education services under the Individuals with Disabilities Education Act (IDEA).

She was referred to this clinic for evaluation of concerns in the following areas: social communication and interaction; adaptive functioning; motor development. A comprehensive diagnostic evaluation was completed at the visit referenced above. Autism Spectrum Disorder was formally considered, and full DSM-5 diagnostic criteria are not met at this evaluation; an autism diagnosis is not being assigned. A single unifying medical diagnosis has not been established. Ruling out autism does not diminish the functional impairments described below, which were directly observed at this evaluation and stand independent of diagnostic labeling. Under IDEA, eligibility turns on whether the district's evaluation identifies one of the disability categories defined at 34 C.F.R. §300.8 and whether, by reason of that disability, the student needs special education and related services. A specific medical diagnosis is neither required for nor sufficient to establish eligibility. In making that determination the team must draw on information from a variety of sources, expressly including information about the student's physical condition and adaptive behavior (34 C.F.R. §300.306(c)(1)(i)), and the evaluation must be sufficiently comprehensive to identify all special education and related services needs, whether or not those needs are commonly linked to the disability category in which the student is classified (34 C.F.R. §300.304(c)(6)).

Speech-language pathology, occupational therapy, and physical therapy are related services available under any IDEA eligibility category (34 C.F.R. §300.34). The communication and motor findings described in this letter support those services regardless of which category the team determines; they are not themselves eligibility categories, and the absence of an autism diagnosis does not bear on them.

Educational eligibility under the Intellectual Disability category should not be assigned on the basis of this physician letter alone. A comprehensive psychoeducational evaluation is required per IDEA evaluation procedures and should include a comprehensive standardized cognitive battery, a standardized adaptive behavior measure (e.g., Vineland-3, ABAS-3, SIB-R, DABS, or BASC-3 adaptive composite), and academic achievement testing.

REQUEST FOR SPECIAL EDUCATION EVALUATION
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
{Student Name} presents with documented functional impairments that adversely affect educational performance, described in the Educational Impact section above. Autism has been ruled out; the need for evaluation has not. Under IDEA's child find requirement, all children with disabilities who are in need of special education and related services must be identified, located, and evaluated, expressly including children suspected of having a disability even though they are advancing from grade to grade (34 C.F.R. §300.111(a)(1)(i) and (c)(1)). Florida implements that requirement through the school districts, which the introductory text of Florida Rule 6A-6.0331 makes responsible for ensuring that all students in need of exceptional student education are identified, located, and evaluated. No medical diagnosis is required to trigger that obligation, and either a parent or the district may initiate a request for an initial evaluation (34 C.F.R. §300.301(b)). I am requesting a comprehensive multidisciplinary evaluation to determine educational eligibility and appropriate services.

Under IDEA (34 C.F.R. §300.301(c)(1)) and Florida Rule 6A-6.0331(3)(g), the school district must complete the initial evaluation within 60 calendar days of receiving written parental consent, excluding the periods the Florida rule specifies (school holidays and breaks, summer vacation, and student absences beyond eight school days). This evaluation should include assessment of: cognitive abilities, academic achievement, adaptive behavior, communication skills, behavioral and social-emotional functioning, and sensory-motor development.

A comprehensive psychoeducational evaluation is recommended to characterize {Student Name}'s academic achievement and cognitive processing profile.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
ELIGIBILITY CATEGORIES FOR THE TEAM TO CONSIDER
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
To help the district assign the right evaluators, the following eligibility categories are the ones this evaluation supports considering. The determination itself belongs to the team; this is a clinical steer, not a conclusion about eligibility.

  • Language Impaired (Florida Rule 6A-6.030121): documented receptive and/or expressive language needs affecting access to instruction. Florida evaluates language and speech eligibility separately under the single federal category of speech or language impairment (34 C.F.R. §300.8(c)(11)).
  • Developmental Delay (Florida Rule 6A-6.03027): Florida makes this category available for children ages three through nine, or through completion of grade 2, whichever occurs first (34 C.F.R. §300.8(b)).

RECOMMENDED SERVICES
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
If eligible for special education services, I recommend the IEP include the following:

Speech-Language Pathology (SLP): School-based SLP services are recommended to address the documented communication needs that directly affect classroom access. Recommended goals include: expressive language development: sentence formulation, topic maintenance, and narrative organization; receptive language: following multi-step classroom directions, understanding figurative language, and processing academic vocabulary.

Occupational Therapy (OT): School-based OT services are recommended to address sensory, fine motor, and/or adaptive needs that affect classroom participation and academic output. Goals: adaptive self-care skills relevant to the school day: cafeteria routines, clothing management, and bathroom independence.

Psychoeducational Evaluation: A comprehensive psychoeducational evaluation is recommended to fully characterize the academic achievement and cognitive processing profile. The evaluation should include: cognitive assessment (IQ and index scores), academic achievement testing across reading, mathematics, and written expression (WIAT-4 or equivalent), phonological processing assessment (CTOPP-2 if reading concerns are present), working memory and processing speed indices, and a Specific Learning Disability (SLD) eligibility determination per Florida Rule 6A-6.03018. Results will directly inform IEP goal development and eligibility determination.

RECOMMENDED ACCOMMODATIONS
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
I recommend that the IEP team consider the following accommodations, calibrated to evaluation findings and individual educational need. These are clinical recommendations, not legal entitlements; the team's selection should reflect what the evaluation supports.

Structure and Predictability Accommodations:
The following accommodations are supported by the specific findings documented in this evaluation. Implementation should be calibrated to this student's developmental level and individual presentation.
  • Visual supports (format calibrated to developmental level and literacy): pictorial schedule and visual transition cues for younger or non-reading students; written agenda, task checklists, or digital planner for older or verbal students — visual representation of schedules, task steps, and expectations reduces transition anxiety and supports regulation for students with communication, attention, and self-regulation needs

Individually Indicated Accommodations Based on Evaluation Findings:
  • Motor accommodations: keyboarding as alternative to handwriting, extended time on written tasks, adapted physical education referral if indicated

Behavioral and Emotional Accommodations:
  • Communication: AAC device access if applicable, visual and written directions in addition to verbal instructions, SLP consultation for classroom communication accommodations
