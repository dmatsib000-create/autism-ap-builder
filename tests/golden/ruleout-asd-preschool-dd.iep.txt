I am a pediatrician at the UF Behavior and Development Clinic. I evaluated {Student Name} on {Date of Evaluation} and am writing to provide a formal medical recommendation in support of the family's request for special education services under the Individuals with Disabilities Education Act (IDEA).

Specifically, I am requesting a comprehensive multidisciplinary evaluation to determine eligibility for special education services. Florida allows 60 calendar days from written parental consent for that evaluation, with the exclusions the rule provides. The findings supporting that request are set out below, together with the eligibility categories I would ask the team to consider and the services and accommodations I recommend.

She was referred to this clinic for evaluation of concerns in the following areas: social communication and interaction; adaptive functioning; motor development. That evaluation did not establish a single unifying medical diagnosis. Autism spectrum disorder was considered and criteria were not met.

The functional impairments described below were directly observed at this evaluation and stand on their own. Under IDEA, eligibility rests on whether the district's evaluation identifies a disability that adversely affects educational performance and creates a need for specially designed instruction; a medical diagnosis is neither required for that determination nor sufficient to establish it (34 C.F.R. §300.8). I hope the findings below are useful to the team, whose evaluation will draw on sources I do not have, including the school's own observations of this student across the day.

Speech-language pathology, occupational therapy, and physical therapy are related services available under any eligibility category (34 C.F.R. §300.34). The communication and motor findings described here support those services whichever category the team determines.

Educational eligibility should not be assigned on the basis of this physician letter alone. Global developmental delay is suspected but not yet confirmed, and the district's evaluation should include a comprehensive standardized developmental assessment; brief developmental screeners are not sufficient to establish the degree of delay. At this age, a developmental evaluation is more informative than an IQ-based cognitive battery or academic achievement testing.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
EDUCATIONAL IMPACT
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
The findings below affect educational performance and require specialized support in the school setting:
  • Communication: Significant difficulty with verbal and nonverbal communication, including understanding classroom instructions, expressing needs, and participating in peer conversation and group activities.
  • Motor skills: Motor difficulties affecting pre-writing and handling of classroom materials, participation in playground and movement activities, and completion of classroom routines requiring fine or gross motor coordination.
  • Adaptive skills: Difficulty with daily living and self-care skills that affect independent functioning within the school setting.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
REQUEST FOR SPECIAL EDUCATION EVALUATION
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
{Student Name} presents with documented functional impairments that affect educational performance, described in the Educational Impact section above. IDEA's child find requirement reaches a student suspected of having a disability even when that student is advancing from grade to grade (34 C.F.R. §300.111(c)(1)), so passing grades need not delay the evaluation.

The evaluation should be a comprehensive developmental evaluation across cognitive, communication, physical, social or emotional, and adaptive development, using a standardized developmental instrument (e.g., the Battelle Developmental Inventory 3rd ed. or the Mullen Scales of Early Learning), together with assessment of behavioral functioning and sensory processing.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
ELIGIBILITY CATEGORIES FOR THE TEAM TO CONSIDER
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
To help the district assign the right evaluators, the following eligibility categories are the ones this evaluation supports considering. The determination itself belongs to the team; this is clinical input, not a conclusion about eligibility.

  • Language Impaired (Florida Rule 6A-6.030121): documented receptive and/or expressive language needs affecting access to instruction. Florida evaluates language and speech eligibility separately, so both may be worth considering.
  • Developmental Delay (Florida Rule 6A-6.03027): Florida makes this category available for children ages three through nine, or through completion of grade 2, whichever occurs first.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
RECOMMENDED SERVICES
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
If eligible for special education services, I recommend the IEP include the following:

Speech-Language Pathology (SLP): School-based SLP services are recommended to address the documented communication needs that affect participation in classroom routines, play, and early learning activities. Recommended goals include: expressive language development: combining words into phrases and longer utterances, and using language to request, comment, protest, and answer simple questions during play and classroom routines; receptive language: following one- and two-step directions within classroom routines, understanding early concepts such as position, size, and quantity (in, on, big, little, more), and responding to simple questions about familiar activities and shared books.

Occupational Therapy (OT): School-based OT services are recommended to address sensory, fine motor, and/or adaptive needs that affect participation in classroom routines, play, and early learning activities. Goals: adaptive self-care skills relevant to the school day: toileting, handwashing, dressing tasks such as jackets and shoes, and snack and mealtime routines.

Developmental Evaluation: A comprehensive developmental evaluation is recommended in place of a standard psychoeducational battery. At this age, a standardized developmental instrument (e.g., the Battelle Developmental Inventory 3rd ed. or the Mullen Scales of Early Learning) covering cognitive, communication, motor, social-emotional, and adaptive development is more informative than IQ or academic achievement testing. Results will directly inform IEP goal development and the Developmental Delay eligibility determination (Florida Rule 6A-6.03027).

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
RECOMMENDED ACCOMMODATIONS
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
I recommend that the IEP team consider the following accommodations, calibrated to evaluation findings and individual educational need. These are clinical recommendations, not legal entitlements; the team's selection should reflect what the evaluation supports.

Structure and Predictability Accommodations:
The following accommodations are supported by the specific findings documented in this evaluation. Implementation should be calibrated to this student's developmental level and individual presentation.
  • Visual supports (format calibrated to developmental level and literacy): pictorial schedule and visual transition cues for younger or non-reading students; written agenda, task checklists, or digital planner for older or verbal students — visual representation of schedules, task steps, and expectations reduces transition anxiety and supports regulation for students with communication, attention, and self-regulation needs

Individually Indicated Accommodations:
  • Motor accommodations: adapted materials (for example, easy-grip crayons, adapted scissors, a slanted work surface), extra time and hands-on support for fine motor tasks, and support for gross motor and playground participation, with physical therapy or adapted physical education consultation if the team finds it indicated

Behavioral and Emotional Accommodations:
  • Communication: AAC device access if applicable, visual and written directions in addition to verbal instructions, SLP consultation for classroom communication accommodations
