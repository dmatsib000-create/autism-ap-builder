RECOMMENDATIONS FOR PRIMARY CARE from Developmental-Behavioral Pediatrics (DBP)

DBP follow-up:
  • No routine DBP visits are planned; you can follow the child with the plan below. This is not a discharge: the child remains in our care and can return as needed. We are glad to see the child and family again for any reason listed under "Re-refer to DBP if". Thank you for caring for this child.
  • How to come back: within 2 years of the last visit, the family or your office can call us directly at (352) 265-8250. No new referral is needed. After 2 years, or if the child was dismissed from our clinic, please send a new referral.

Actions:
  • PCP (next visit): Ask whether ABA, speech-language therapy, parent-child interaction therapy (PCIT), and a social skills group have started. If not, ask where the family is on each waitlist. The insurance case manager can find in-network providers, and families often have to call a waitlist more than once.
  • PCP (next visit): Check that these referrals are scheduled or done: clinical genetics and pediatric gastroenterology. We placed every referral. Some insurance plans want the referral to come from primary care; if so, please re-send it. You do not need to order genetic testing: clinical genetics orders it and follows up the results.
  • Family (now): Ask the school district in writing for a special education evaluation. Once the family signs consent, the district has 60 days to evaluate.
  • PCP (in 4 to 6 weeks): Ask how sleep is going. Keep the behavioral sleep plan going, with melatonin at the dose in our plan. If snoring, pauses in breathing, or restless sleep appear, order a sleep study.
  • PCP (well visits): Autism is more likely in younger siblings. Screen them for autism at the 18- and 24-month visits, and refer early for any concern.
  • PCP (well visits): Ask the family how their wandering and water-safety precautions are working.

Primary care manages:
  • GI symptoms: Treat constipation with polyethylene glycol dosed by weight. In this child, a change in behavior or sleep may be the only sign of abdominal pain.

Re-refer to DBP if:
  • A new developmental, learning, attention, or mood concern needs a diagnostic evaluation.
  • Self-injury, aggression, or unsafe behavior continues despite current supports, or you are considering medication for behavior.
  • School, insurance, disability services, or the move to adult care needs updated diagnostic testing or documentation.
  • Sleep problems continue after the behavioral plan and a melatonin trial.

Urgent, or for another service:
  • The child loses language, social, or motor skills they already had: refer to pediatric neurology.
