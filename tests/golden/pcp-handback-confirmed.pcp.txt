RECOMMENDATIONS FOR PRIMARY CARE

Actions:
  • PCP (next visit): Confirm that ABA, speech-language therapy, PCIT, and a social skills group have started. If not, the family should ask the insurance case manager for in-network providers and keep calling the waitlists.
  • PCP (next visit): Confirm that these referrals are scheduled or completed: clinical genetics and pediatric gastroenterology. Clinical genetics orders and follows the genetic testing, so none needs to be ordered in primary care.
  • Family (now): Send the school district a written request for a special education evaluation. The district must evaluate within 60 days of written consent.
  • PCP (well visits): Watch the development of younger siblings closely, with autism screening at the 18- and 24-month visits and early referral for any concern. Autism is more likely in siblings.
  • PCP (well visits): Revisit wandering and water-safety precautions with the family.

Primary care can monitor and manage (no routine DBP follow-up needed for these):
  • Sleep: Continue the behavioral sleep plan, with melatonin as outlined in the plan, and reassess in 4 to 6 weeks. Arrange a sleep study if snoring, pauses in breathing, or restless sleep appear.
  • GI symptoms: Treat constipation in primary care with polyethylene glycol dosed by weight. A change in behavior or sleep may be the only sign of abdominal pain.

DBP follow-up:
  • No routine DBP follow-up is needed. Thank you for continuing this child's care. Primary care follows from here, and we are always glad to see the family again for any of the changes listed below.
  • To come back: within 2 years of the last visit here, please call our clinic directly at {clinic phone number}; no new referral is needed. After 2 years, or if the child has been discharged from our clinic, please send a new referral.

Re-refer to DBP if:
  • A new developmental, learning, attention, or mood concern needs diagnostic evaluation.
  • Self-injury, aggression, or unsafe behavior persists despite the current supports, or medication for behavior is being considered.
  • Updated diagnostic testing or documentation is required for school, insurance, disability services, or the move to adult care.
  • Sleep problems persist after the behavioral plan and a melatonin trial.

Refer directly to another service if:
  • Language, social, or motor skills the child already had are lost: refer to pediatric neurology.
