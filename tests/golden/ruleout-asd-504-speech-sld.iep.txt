Specifically, I am requesting that the team review the current 504 Plan and consider whether {Student Name} now meets eligibility for an Individualized Education Program under IDEA. The findings supporting that request are set out below, together with the eligibility categories I would ask the team to consider and the services and accommodations I recommend.

He was referred to this clinic for evaluation of concerns in the following areas: social communication and interaction; adaptive functioning; motor development. That evaluation documented a specific learning disability in reading (dyslexia), which affects educational performance. Autism spectrum disorder was considered and criteria were not met.

The functional impairments described below were directly observed at this evaluation and stand on their own. Under IDEA, eligibility rests on whether the district's evaluation identifies a disability that adversely affects educational performance and creates a need for specially designed instruction; a medical diagnosis is neither required for that determination nor sufficient to establish it (34 C.F.R. §300.8). I hope the findings below are useful to the team, whose evaluation will draw on sources I do not have, including the school's own observations of this student across the day.

Speech-language pathology, occupational therapy, and physical therapy are related services available under any eligibility category (34 C.F.R. §300.34). The communication and motor findings described here support those services whichever category the team determines.

REQUEST FOR IEP ELIGIBILITY EVALUATION AND/OR 504 REVIEW
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
I understand that {Student Name} currently has a 504 Plan in place. Based on the current evaluation, I am requesting that the team consider: (1) updating the 504 Plan to reflect current functional needs, and (2) evaluating whether {Student Name} meets eligibility for an Individualized Education Program (IEP) under IDEA, which provides more comprehensive and legally enforceable services and supports than a 504 accommodation plan.

Given the identified level of support needed, a 504 accommodation plan alone may be insufficient to provide Free and Appropriate Public Education (FAPE) as defined under IDEA and Florida Rule 6A-6.03028(1). IDEA eligibility does not turn on any particular medical diagnosis, and the functional impairments documented above are substantial. I recommend the team evaluate IEP eligibility under the categories identified below.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
ELIGIBILITY CATEGORIES FOR THE TEAM TO CONSIDER
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
To help the district assign the right evaluators, the following eligibility categories are the ones this evaluation supports considering. The determination itself belongs to the team; this is clinical input, not a conclusion about eligibility.

  • Language Impaired (Florida Rule 6A-6.030121): documented receptive and/or expressive language needs affecting access to instruction. Florida evaluates language and speech eligibility separately, so both may be worth considering.
  • Speech Impaired (Florida Rule 6A-6.03012): documented speech-sound production and/or intelligibility needs, evaluated separately from language eligibility.
  • Specific Learning Disability (Florida Rule 6A-6.03018): documented learning disability affecting one or more academic domains.
  • Intellectual Disability (Florida Rule 6A-6.03011): cognitive and adaptive findings documented at this evaluation warrant the district's own eligibility determination.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
RECOMMENDED SERVICES
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
If eligible for special education services, I recommend the IEP include the following:

Special Education (Specialized Academic Instruction): Specially designed instruction, delivered by a qualified ESE teacher, is recommended to address academic goals aligned with the identified areas of need. Specialized academic instruction should be individually tailored to the student's present levels of performance and updated at each annual IEP review.

Speech-Language Pathology (SLP): School-based SLP services are recommended to address the documented communication needs that directly affect classroom access. Recommended goals include: expressive language development: sentence formulation, topic maintenance, and narrative organization; speech intelligibility and articulation targeting sounds that reduce classroom communication effectiveness.

Occupational Therapy (OT): School-based OT services are recommended to address sensory, fine motor, and/or adaptive needs that affect classroom participation and academic output. Goals: fine motor and graphomotor development: pencil grip, letter formation, and/or keyboarding as a compensatory strategy for written output; adaptive self-care skills relevant to the school day: cafeteria routines, clothing management, and bathroom independence.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
RECOMMENDED ACCOMMODATIONS
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
I recommend that the IEP team consider the following accommodations, calibrated to evaluation findings and individual educational need. These are clinical recommendations, not legal entitlements; the team's selection should reflect what the evaluation supports.

Structure and Predictability Accommodations:
The following accommodations are supported by the specific findings documented in this evaluation. Implementation should be calibrated to this student's developmental level and individual presentation.
  • Predictable daily schedule with advance written or visual notice before transitions (schedule format and complexity calibrated to developmental level)
  • Visual supports (format calibrated to developmental level and literacy): pictorial schedule and visual transition cues for younger or non-reading students; written agenda, task checklists, or digital planner for older or verbal students — visual representation of schedules, task steps, and expectations reduces transition anxiety and supports regulation for students with communication, attention, and self-regulation needs

Individually Indicated Accommodations:
  • Motor accommodations: keyboarding as alternative to handwriting, extended time on written tasks, adapted physical education referral if indicated

Behavioral and Emotional Accommodations:
  • Communication: AAC device access if applicable, visual and written directions in addition to verbal instructions, SLP consultation for classroom communication accommodations

Academic Accommodations:
  • Reading: text-to-speech technology, audiobooks for reading-dependent assignments, extended time on reading tasks, oral response option
