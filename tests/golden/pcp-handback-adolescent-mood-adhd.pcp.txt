RECOMMENDATIONS FOR PRIMARY CARE from Developmental-Behavioral Pediatrics (DBP)

DBP follow-up:
  • No routine DBP follow-up is planned. The patient has not been discharged and can return as needed. We are glad to see the patient and family again for any reason listed under "Re-refer to DBP if". Thank you for continuing this patient's care.
  • To come back: within 2 years of the last visit here, the family or primary care can call our clinic directly at (352) 265-8250; no new referral is needed. After 2 years, or if the patient has been dismissed from our clinic, please send a new referral.

Actions:
  • PCP (next visit): Confirm that ABA, speech-language therapy, psychotherapy, and a social skills group have started. If not, ask where the family is on each waitlist and point them to the insurance case manager for in-network providers; waitlists often need repeated calls.
  • PCP (next visit): Confirm that this referral is scheduled or completed: clinical genetics. Our clinic placed every referral; some insurance plans need one re-sent from primary care. Clinical genetics orders and follows the genetic testing, so none needs to be ordered in primary care.
  • PCP (in 4 to 6 weeks, sooner if mood worsens): Recheck mood and safety. Screen for suicide risk with the NIMH ASQ (Ask Suicide-Screening Questions, validated from age 10). For a younger child, or one who does not use speech to answer, ask the child in plain, concrete words in the way the child communicates, and ask the caregiver what has changed. A negative screen does not override caregiver or clinician concern. If the screen is positive, complete the ASQ Brief Suicide Safety Assessment (BSSA) before the patient leaves, following the NIMH pathway for outpatient primary care and specialty clinics, then follow the suicide line under "Urgent, or for another service".
  • PCP (now): Start health care transition planning with the family: adult providers, decision-making supports, and benefits.

Primary care manages:
  • ADHD medication: Our clinic adjusts the dose until it is stable. Primary care prescribes and monitors from {now, or the date DBP confirms a stable dose}. Check height, weight, blood pressure, heart rate, sleep, appetite, and mood or irritability at each medication visit. Children with autism have more stimulant side effects than children with ADHD alone, so any later dose change is made slowly.

Re-refer to DBP if:
  • A new developmental, learning, attention, or mood concern needs diagnostic evaluation.
  • Self-injury, aggression, or unsafe behavior persists despite the current supports, or medication for behavior is being considered.
  • Updated diagnostic testing or documentation is required for school, insurance, disability services, or the move to adult care.
  • ADHD symptoms remain impairing despite treatment.
  • Depression is not improving with treatment, or is worsening.

Urgent, or for another service:
  • Suicidal intent, a plan, or an attempt: emergency evaluation the same day. Suicidal thoughts without intent or a plan: complete the ASQ Brief Suicide Safety Assessment at that visit, make a written safety plan with the patient and family, and ask the family to lock up firearms and medications. When the assessment calls for mental health evaluation within 72 hours, options in our area are: voluntary psychiatric evaluation at UF Psychiatric Hospital (Vista), Meridian, or SMA in other counties; Charlie Health (telehealth intensive outpatient therapy and psychiatry, usually a rapid intake, age 11 and older); or a Community Action Treatment (CAT) team for a child at high risk of hospitalization who does not meet criteria for involuntary admission (352-505-3534; myflfamilies.com/catt). The family can call or text 988 at any time.
  • New mania or psychosis: mental health evaluation the same day (emergency department if none is available), and hold any stimulant until then. Substance use that complicates treatment: refer to child psychiatry.
  • Language, social, or motor skills the patient already had are lost: refer to pediatric neurology.
