RECOMMENDATIONS FOR PRIMARY CARE from Developmental-Behavioral Pediatrics (DBP)

DBP follow-up:
  • No routine DBP visits are planned; you can follow the patient with the plan below. This is not a discharge: the patient remains in our care and can return as needed. We are glad to see the patient and family again for any reason listed under "Re-refer to DBP if". Thank you for caring for this patient.
  • How to come back: within 2 years of the last visit, the family or your office can call us directly at (352) 265-8250. No new referral is needed. After 2 years, or if the patient was dismissed from our clinic, please send a new referral.

Actions:
  • PCP (next visit): Ask whether ABA, speech-language therapy, psychotherapy, and a social skills group have started. If not, ask where the family is on each waitlist. The insurance case manager can find in-network providers, and families often have to call a waitlist more than once.
  • PCP (next visit): Check that this referral is scheduled or done: clinical genetics. We placed every referral. Some insurance plans want the referral to come from primary care; if so, please re-send it. You do not need to order genetic testing: clinical genetics orders it and follows up the results.
  • PCP (in 4 to 6 weeks, sooner if mood worsens): Recheck mood and safety with the ASQ (Ask Suicide-Screening Questions). NIMH made this screen for medical clinics; the free NIMH ASQ toolkit (search "NIMH ASQ toolkit") has the questions, a script, and a worksheet for each step.
      1. For a patient 10 or older, ask the 4 yes/no questions, ideally with the parent out of the room. It takes about 20 seconds.
      2. If every answer is no, the screen is negative. Still act on your own concern or the caregiver's; a negative screen does not outweigh it.
      3. If any answer is yes, do the Brief Suicide Safety Assessment (BSSA) before the patient leaves. It takes about 10 to 15 minutes, and the toolkit worksheet walks you through it. Then follow "If suicidal thoughts come up" under "Urgent, or for another service".
      For a younger child, or one who does not use speech to answer, ask about safety in plain, concrete words, in the way the child communicates, and ask the caregiver what has changed.
  • PCP (now): Start planning the move to adult health care with the family: adult doctors, support for decision-making, and benefits.

Primary care manages:
  • ADHD medication: We adjust the dose until it is stable. You take over prescribing from {now, or the date DBP confirms a stable dose}. At each medication visit, check height, weight, blood pressure, heart rate, sleep, appetite, and mood or irritability. Children with autism get more stimulant side effects than children with ADHD alone, so make any later dose change slowly.

Re-refer to DBP if:
  • A new developmental, learning, attention, or mood concern needs a diagnostic evaluation.
  • Self-injury, aggression, or unsafe behavior continues despite current supports, or you are considering medication for behavior.
  • School, insurance, disability services, or the move to adult care needs updated diagnostic testing or documentation.
  • ADHD symptoms still get in the way despite treatment.
  • Depression is not improving with treatment, or is worsening.

Urgent, or for another service:
  • If suicidal thoughts come up:
      - Intent, a plan, or an attempt: arrange an emergency evaluation the same day.
      - Thoughts without intent or a plan: do the BSSA at that visit. Make a written safety plan with the patient and family, and ask the family to lock up firearms and medications.
      - If the BSSA calls for a mental health evaluation within 72 hours, options in our area are: voluntary psychiatric evaluation at UF Psychiatric Hospital (Vista), Meridian, or SMA in other counties; Charlie Health (telehealth intensive outpatient therapy and psychiatry, usually a quick intake, age 11 and older); or a Community Action Treatment (CAT) team, for a child at high risk of hospitalization who does not meet criteria for involuntary admission (352-505-3534; myflfamilies.com/catt).
      - Give every patient who screens positive the toolkit's patient resource list. The family can call or text 988 at any time.
  • New mania or psychosis: arrange a mental health evaluation the same day (the emergency department if none is available), and hold any stimulant until then. Substance use that complicates treatment: refer to child psychiatry.
  • The patient loses language, social, or motor skills they already had: refer to pediatric neurology.
