── Problem 2: Persistent Toe Walking (R26.89) ──

Assessment:
  Persistent toe walking reported. Toe walking is considered idiopathic only when it is bilateral, present from the start of walking, persists beyond age 2 years, and has no neurologic cause; co-occurring autism or ADHD does not exclude it.¹ Toe walking is more common in autistic children and is more likely to persist without treatment than in other children.²,³
  Examination and history: heel-cord contracture (cannot dorsiflex to neutral). {specify the findings present}
  A tight heel cord alone does not indicate a neurologic cause.⁴

Plan:
  • PT: evaluation and treatment of toe walking, with stretching, strengthening, and splinting or serial casting as the therapist judges appropriate. Evidence for these treatments is of very low certainty, and adding botulinum toxin to casting has not shown clear benefit.⁵
  • Family counseling: toe walking may persist or return after treatment, including in autistic children.¹,³
  • Orthopaedics: if the ankle does not reach neutral after a course of PT, or toe walking persists, refer to pediatric orthopaedics for serial casting or surgical evaluation.¹ Casting and surgery improve ankle range, but gait does not fully normalize and functional benefit is unproven.⁶
