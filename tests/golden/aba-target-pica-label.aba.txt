LETTER OF MEDICAL NECESSITY
Applied Behavior Analysis (ABA) Therapy — Autism Spectrum Disorder

Date: [DATE]
Diagnosis: Autism Spectrum Disorder, Level 2 — Requiring Substantial Support (DSM-5)
ICD-10-CM: F84.0

To Whom It May Concern / Dear Medical Director:

I write to request authorization for Applied Behavior Analysis (ABA) therapy for the patient, who has received a confirmed diagnosis of Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD), Level 2 — Requiring Substantial Support, as defined by the Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders, Fifth Edition (DSM-5; American Psychiatric Association, 2013) enrolled under commercial/private insurance. ABA therapy is medically necessary, as documented below.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
I. DIAGNOSIS & LEVEL OF SUPPORT
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Marked deficits in verbal and nonverbal social communication skills are present; social impairments are apparent even with support in place. They exhibit behavioral inflexibility, difficulty coping with change, and restricted/repetitive behaviors that appear with sufficient frequency to be observable to others and that interfere with their functioning across multiple settings. ABA therapy at adequate intensity is medically necessary to address these substantial support needs, build foundational communication and adaptive skills, and reduce the frequency and severity of interfering behaviors.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
II. FUNCTIONAL IMPAIRMENT
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
They demonstrate clinically significant functional impairments across the following domains: behavioral regulation and reduction of interfering behaviors; safety awareness and risk management. With respect to communication, they use simple sentences. Of particular urgency, they exhibit self-injurious behavior (SIB) and pica (ingestion of non-food items), which further establish the medical necessity of ABA intervention with trained behavioral specialists who can implement evidence-based safety protocols and individualized behavioral support plans.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
III. TREATMENT NECESSITY & EVIDENCE BASE
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
ABA therapy, the most extensively studied behavioral intervention for ASD, is explicitly recommended as a primary evidence-based treatment by the American Academy of Pediatrics (AAP).¹ Multiple federal agency systematic reviews confirm its efficacy across communication, adaptive behavior, and quality-of-life outcomes.²

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
IV. REQUESTED TREATMENT PARAMETERS
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Requested intensity: 20 hours per week
Priority treatment targets:
  • Reduction of pica (ingestion of non-food items)
  • Reduction of self-injurious behavior (SIB)

Treatment will be implemented by a Board Certified Behavior Analyst (BCBA) who will oversee program development, conduct ongoing assessment, and adjust treatment based on objective data. Direct therapy will be provided by trained Registered Behavior Technicians (RBTs) under BCBA supervision, consistent with BACB professional and ethical standards.
