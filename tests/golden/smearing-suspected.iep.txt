He has received a diagnosis of Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD) (ICD-10-CM: F84.0), classified as Level 2 (requiring substantial support), confirmed at the evaluation referenced above.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
EDUCATIONAL IMPACT
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
These conditions result in the following challenges that require specialized support in the educational setting:
  • Behavior: Toileting and personal-hygiene needs requiring structured support, scheduled opportunities, and a consistent, dignity-preserving staff response.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
REQUEST FOR SPECIAL EDUCATION EVALUATION
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Based on the above diagnosis and identified educational impact, I strongly recommend that {Student Name} receive a comprehensive evaluation for special education eligibility under IDEA, including consideration of the Autism Spectrum Disorder eligibility category (34 C.F.R. §300.8(c)(1); Florida Rule 6A-6.03023) and any additional eligibility categories supported by the evaluation results.

The evaluation should include assessment of: cognitive abilities, academic achievement, adaptive behavior, communication skills, behavioral and social-emotional functioning, and sensory-motor development.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
RECOMMENDED ACCOMMODATIONS
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
I recommend that the IEP team consider the following accommodations, calibrated to evaluation findings and individual educational need. These are clinical recommendations, not legal entitlements; the team's selection should reflect what the evaluation supports.

Core ASD-Related Accommodations:
The following accommodations have broad evidence support across the autism spectrum. Implementation should be calibrated to this student's developmental level and individual presentation.
  • Predictable daily schedule with advance written or visual notice before transitions (schedule format and complexity calibrated to developmental level)
  • Visual supports (format calibrated to developmental level and literacy): pictorial schedule and visual transition cues for younger or non-reading students; written agenda, task checklists, or digital planner for older or verbal students — visual representation of schedules, task steps, and expectations reduces transition anxiety and supports regulation across the autism spectrum

Behavioral and Emotional Accommodations:
  • Toileting and hygiene support: a consistent, private toileting and clean-up routine with visual step-by-step supports; proactively scheduled bathroom breaks rather than waiting for requests; staff trained to respond calmly, matter-of-factly, and without drawing peer attention; coordination with the student's medical bowel-management plan; a behavior support plan addressing the function identified through functional behavior assessment; incidents handled discreetly and communicated with the family to preserve the student's dignity
