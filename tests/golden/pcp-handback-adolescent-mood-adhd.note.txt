Plan:
  Treatment is indicated based on the functional impairment documented in the Assessment above.
  Medication approach in ASD+ADHD differs from standard ADHD care: only ~49% of ASD children respond to methylphenidate (vs. higher rates in non-autistic ADHD); adverse effect rates are higher. Non-stimulants, particularly guanfacine ER, have RCT support in ASD and may be preferred as first-line agents in many patients.
  • Non-stimulants (consider first-line in ASD):
    - Guanfacine ER: start 1 mg daily; increase by 1 mg/week to maximum 4 mg/day. Has RCT evidence in ASD (50% positive response; also benefits irritability, anxiety, sleep, tics). Additional benefit if sleep or anxiety co-occurring.
    - Clonidine ER: start 0.1 mg at night; increase weekly by 0.1 mg to maximum 0.4 mg/day. More sedating than guanfacine; useful if sleep-onset is a target.
    - Atomoxetine: start 0.5 mg/kg/day; titrate to 1.2–1.4 mg/kg/day (max 100 mg/day). Black box warning: suicidal ideation; monitor closely.
  • Stimulants (if non-stimulant insufficient or not tolerated):
    - Methylphenidate IR: start 2.5 mg AM; increase by 2.5 mg every 5–7 days. Maximum 35 mg/day (≤25 kg) or 60 mg/day (>25 kg).
    - Amphetamine salts IR: start 2.5 mg; maximum 40 mg/day (or 60 mg/day if >50 kg). No RCTs in ASD; extrapolated from ADHD data.
    - ER formulations preferred once effective IR dose established.
  • Cardiac screening before initiation (history and exam; ECG if indicated).
  • Monitor at each titration step: appetite, weight/growth, sleep onset, mood/irritability, and stereotypy. Note: ASD patients are less likely to self-report adverse effects; behavioral change is the signal.
  • Vanderbilt or Conners Rating Scales (teacher and parent forms) at each titration visit to quantify response.
  • IEP/504 accommodations (extended time, preferential seating, chunked assignments) regardless of medication status.
  • Psychiatry co-management for medication management; via existing referral or new referral if not already placed.

  Treatment is indicated based on the functional impairment documented in the Assessment above.
  • Psychotherapy first-line: Behavioral Activation therapy (BA) or modified CBT adapted for ASD; requires therapist with both depression and ASD training.
  Pharmacotherapy for depression in ASD: no RCTs of depression treatment specifically in ASD exist. SSRIs (while first-line in neurotypical pediatric depression) have not shown consistent efficacy in ASD and carry high risk of behavioral activation syndrome (agitation, hyperactivity, or impulsivity). ASD-specific preferred agents:
  • Duloxetine (SNRI; preferred first-line if comorbid anxiety or pain): start 20 mg daily; increase by 10 mg every 1-2 weeks; range 20-90 mg/day in 1-2 divided doses. Addresses co-occurring anxiety and depression simultaneously. Black box warning: suicidality; monitor closely.
  • Mirtazapine (preferred if sleep disruption, appetite loss, or anxiety co-occurring): start 3.75 mg at bedtime; increase by 3.75 mg weekly; maximum 45 mg/day. Common adverse effects: drowsiness, weight gain.
  • Bupropion (if ADHD symptoms co-occurring or SSRI/SNRI not tolerated): start 150 mg ER daily; may increase to 300 mg/day after ≥7 days (FDA adult label; pediatric practice typically extends to 7-14 days; typically reserved for school-age children and adolescents — off-label; exercise caution in children under 12). Does not cause weight gain or sexual dysfunction. Caution: lowers seizure threshold; avoid if epilepsy co-occurring; activating and may worsen anxiety.
  • SSRIs (second-line, after above agents fail): fluoxetine has strongest neurotypical pediatric RCT evidence but monitor closely for behavioral activation syndrome (agitation, hyperactivity, disinhibition) in ASD at each dose change; black box warning for suicidality counseled. If behavioral activation occurs: hold the dose increase, return to the prior tolerated dose, and contact the prescribing provider within 24-48 hours.
  • Psychiatry referral: strongly recommended for medication management and ongoing safety monitoring.
  • Mood and safety recheck in 4–6 weeks with primary care.
