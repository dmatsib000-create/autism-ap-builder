      Current society guidance recommends that first-tier evaluation for unexplained neurodevelopmental disorders include exome or genome sequencing (ES/GS) together with chromosomal microarray (CMA), sequential or concurrent, with trio analysis when feasible.¹,² Fragile X (FMR1) testing and screening for treatable inborn errors of metabolism are recommended at the second tier in apparently nonsyndromic cases, or earlier if phenotype or family history is suggestive.¹
      Establishing a specific genetic etiology supports prognostication, surveillance for disorder-related complications, accurate recurrence risk for the family, eligibility for disease-specific services and clinical trials, and (for treatable inborn errors of metabolism) directed therapy.¹
      Per scope of practice, our clinic does not perform genetic consent, pretest counseling, or ordering of specialized genetic testing; these are coordinated by clinical genetics, including testing selection, dysmorphology examination, specimen collection, and order entry. Venipuncture for limited testing (Fragile X, CMA) is available at our clinic but is deferred on compassionate grounds, as it is poorly tolerated in children with autism and developmental delays; centralizing specimen collection through clinical genetics avoids repeated traumatic blood draws.
