EDUCATIONAL IMPACT
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
These conditions result in the following challenges that require specialized support in the educational setting:
  • Communication: Significant difficulty with verbal and nonverbal communication, including understanding classroom instructions, expressing needs, and participating in peer conversation and group activities.
  • Social understanding: Difficulty with social comprehension, perspective-taking, and peer interaction that limits meaningful participation in group academic and social activities.
  • Behavior: Behavioral safety concerns requiring active supervision and impacting the safety of the student and others in the environment; elopement risk requiring direct supervision at all times, limiting ability to participate in group or less-structured activities; difficulty with emotional regulation and behavioral compliance that interrupts instruction for this student and peers; inflexibility with routine changes and transitions that causes significant distress and limits engagement with non-preferred tasks; rigidity is manifest in part through restricted or fixated interests that limit flexible engagement with non-preferred curriculum topics and transitions between subjects.
  • Sensory processing: Sensory processing differences that interfere with the ability to sustain attention, remain regulated, and access the curriculum in a typical classroom environment.
  • Motor skills: Motor difficulties affecting handwriting, physical education participation, and completion of classroom tasks requiring fine or gross motor coordination.
  • Adaptive skills: Difficulty with daily living and self-care skills that affect independent functioning within the school setting.

RECOMMENDED SERVICES
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
I recommend the following updates to the current IEP:

Special Education (Specialized Academic Instruction): Specially designed instruction, delivered by a qualified ESE teacher, is recommended to address academic goals aligned with the identified areas of need. Specialized academic instruction should be individually tailored to the student's present levels of performance and updated at each annual IEP review.

Speech-Language Pathology (SLP): School-based SLP services are recommended to address the documented communication needs that directly affect classroom access. Recommended goals include: AAC system evaluation and implementation (low-tech: core vocabulary boards, PODD; and/or high-tech: speech-generating device) with training for all school communication partners; expressive language development: sentence formulation, topic maintenance, and narrative organization; receptive language: following multi-step classroom directions, understanding figurative language, and processing academic vocabulary; social-pragmatic language: conversational turn-taking, perspective-taking in academic discussion, and understanding classroom social discourse.

Occupational Therapy (OT): School-based OT services are recommended to address sensory, fine motor, and/or adaptive needs that affect classroom participation and academic output. Goals: sensory support plan (Sensory Profile 2 and/or SPM-2 evaluation): individualized sensory regulation strategies implemented in the classroom in coordination with the teacher; motor planning and praxis: task sequencing, tool use, and coordination of multi-step school tasks; adaptive self-care skills relevant to the school day: cafeteria routines, clothing management, and bathroom independence.

Social Skills Instruction: Structured, autism-specific social skills instruction within the school setting is recommended. Goals: peer interaction initiation, conversational reciprocity, cooperative work skills, and understanding and navigating social expectations in group academic and recess settings. At this developmental stage, goals should also address social communication in vocational and community contexts.

Paraprofessional Support (1:1 Aide): A dedicated paraprofessional is recommended based on the documented support needs identified in this evaluation. Specific rationale: active elopement risk requiring continuous proximity supervision; behavioral safety needs requiring capacity for immediate adult intervention; significant adaptive skill deficits requiring adult support throughout the school day. The aide's role and prompting hierarchy should be explicitly specified in the IEP to promote independence and prevent prompt dependency.

Extended School Year (ESY): ESY services are recommended based on the documented risk of significant skill regression during extended breaks from school. Students with ASD at this level of need are at elevated risk for regression in communication, behavioral regulation, and adaptive skills during summer break, with evidence of disproportionate recovery time upon return to the school year. ESY eligibility and services should be formally determined by the IEP team based on documented regression data; this clinical evaluation supports the team's consideration of ESY services.

Low Student-to-Teacher Ratio Classroom: A small group or self-contained classroom setting with a low student-to-teacher ratio is recommended. The structured, lower-stimulation environment of a small classroom supports sensory regulation, individualized instructional pacing, and behavioral consistency that is not reliably achievable in a general education classroom of typical size without highly intensive support.

Visual Supports and Visual Schedules: Visual supports are a research-supported, ASD-specific accommodation that significantly improve predictability, independence, and behavioral regulation. Recommended visual supports include: a daily visual schedule (individual desk copy and classroom display), visual task sequences for multi-step activities, visual transition warnings (timer with visual cue), and visual rules and expectations posted in the classroom. Visual supports should be developed in collaboration with the SLP and/or OT and implemented consistently across all school settings including specials, lunch, and recess.

Functional Behavior Assessment (FBA): An FBA is recommended to identify the function (purpose) of interfering behaviors and to develop an individualized, function-based Behavior Intervention Plan (BIP). Behaviors warranting FBA: aggression toward others, self-injurious behavior, elopement/wandering, task refusal and noncompliance, rigidity and difficulty with transitions. I recommend that the FBA be conducted by a Board-Certified Behavior Analyst (BCBA) or a school-based behavior specialist with documented training in functional behavior assessment, and include direct observation, ABC data collection, and structured interviews with caregivers and teachers. The resulting BIP should be implemented consistently by all school staff and reviewed regularly by the IEP team based on collected data, with formal review recommended at minimum every quarter.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
RECOMMENDED ACCOMMODATIONS
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
I recommend that the IEP team consider the following accommodations, calibrated to evaluation findings and individual educational need. These are clinical recommendations, not legal entitlements; the team's selection should reflect what the evaluation supports.

Core ASD-Related Accommodations (implementation calibrated to developmental level and individual presentation):
  • Predictable daily schedule with advance written or visual notice before transitions (schedule format and complexity calibrated to developmental level)
  • Visual supports (format calibrated to developmental level and literacy): pictorial schedule and visual transition cues for younger or non-reading students; written agenda, task checklists, or digital planner for older or verbal students — visual representation of schedules, task steps, and expectations reduces transition anxiety and supports regulation across the autism spectrum

Individually Indicated Accommodations Based on Evaluation Findings:
  • Sensory accommodations: reduced auditory and visual stimulation as needed; scheduled movement breaks; sensory toolkit (noise-canceling headphones, fidget tools, sunglasses); alternative seating options (wiggle cushion, standing desk)
  • Motor accommodations: keyboarding as alternative to handwriting, extended time on written tasks, adapted physical education referral if indicated

Behavioral and Emotional Accommodations:
  • Attention: extended time on assessments and lengthy assignments, preferential seating away from distractors, chunked assignments with check-ins, movement breaks
  • Anxiety: designated calm-down space, graduated support for anxiety triggers, flexible deadlines for anxiety-driven avoidance, advance notice before schedule changes
  • Communication: AAC device access if applicable, visual and written directions in addition to verbal instructions, SLP consultation for classroom communication accommodations
  • Feeding and mealtime: permission to bring preferred foods from home; no staff pressure to try unfamiliar or non-preferred foods; extended mealtime time to accommodate selectivity or slow intake; cafeteria seating away from high-noise areas where possible; teacher awareness that food selectivity in ASD is sensory-based and not volitional
  • Transition supports: individual advance warning before all transitions (verbal cue plus visual timer, minimum 5 minutes prior); visual schedule available at desk and on classroom board showing the full day sequence, with notation of any deviations from routine; first-then board or transition object to support predictability during activity changes; extended transition time built into schedule between high-demand activities; designated consistent staff greeter at morning arrival to establish routine and support regulation at the start of the school day; structured and consistent arrival and dismissal routine (same staff, same location, same sequence each day); early release from class before hallway crowding at dismissal to reduce sensory and transition load; parent-school daily communication system to flag morning home state that may affect school regulation (e.g., brief arrival note, communication app, or teacher check-in at drop-off)
